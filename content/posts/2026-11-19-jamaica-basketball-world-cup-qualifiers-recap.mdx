The Jamaica national basketball team's run in the FIBA Basketball World Cup 2027 Americas Qualifiers ended in July. Jamaica started with two wins in San Juan, then lost its last four games. It finished 2-4 in Group B and missed the second round on points difference.

It was Jamaica's first appearance in World Cup qualifiers. The team reached them by finishing second in its pre-qualifying group in Mexico in August 2025, a run that included [Norman Powell's 34 points in an overtime win over Costa Rica](/posts/norman-powell-jamaica-debut-34-points-costa-rica).

## November: a 2-0 start on the road

Jamaica was meant to host the opener, but the Jamaica Basketball Association (JABA) failed a FIBA venue inspection in September 2025, and FIBA reported that Hurricane Melissa forced both games to San Juan. Jamaica won them anyway. Kofi Cockburn finished at the buzzer to win the first game 92-90, finishing with 26 points and 15 rebounds. Chase Audige scored 20 as Jamaica won the second 97-92.

Jamaica became the first team in these qualifiers to score 90 or more in each of its first two games. [Read the full Puerto Rico story](/posts/jamaica-stuns-puerto-rico-fiba-qualifiers).

## February and March: two close calls at home

Jamaica's first official home games were also the first top-level international basketball games at the National Indoor Sports Centre. A win over the Bahamas on February 26 would have secured a place in the next round. Jamaica led 81-77 after three quarters, but the Bahamas won the fourth 22-15. Franco Miller scored 32 points for the Bahamas, and Cockburn scored 23 for Jamaica.

Canada won 97-81 three days later. Jamaica went into the final window at 2-2. [Read more on the home games](/posts/jamaica-2026-world-cup-qualifiers).

## July: Nassau and Hamilton

**Bahamas 123, Jamaica 74.** The Bahamas led for all but 46 seconds at the Sir Kendal Isaacs Gymnasium. They were up 61-32 at halftime and 90-48 after three quarters. VJ Edgecombe of the Philadelphia 76ers scored 26 points with nine rebounds and five steals. Dominic Bridgewater added 23 off the bench, and five Bahamians reached double figures. Andrew Thelwell led Jamaica with 24 points and Audige added 18.

"We just got punched in the mouth by a really, really good basketball team," head coach Rick Turner told the *Jamaica Observer*.

**Canada 116, Jamaica 78.** Three days later at TD Coliseum in Hamilton, Canada finished the first round 6-0. Jamaica led 3-0 early, but Canada took the lead for good in the first quarter and forced 19 turnovers. Dillon Brooks scored 20, Andrew Nembhard 17 and Shai Gilgeous-Alexander 16 in just over 20 minutes in his hometown. Thelwell scored a game-high 25 for Jamaica.

## How Jamaica missed out

The top three teams in the group advanced. Puerto Rico's 115-92 win over the Bahamas left Jamaica, the Bahamas and Puerto Rico level at 2-4. Head-to-head results couldn't separate them, so points difference decided it. Jamaica's was -99 after the two July defeats, against -18 for the Bahamas and -16 for Puerto Rico. Canada, the Bahamas and Puerto Rico went through.

## What Turner said next

Turner described the campaign as a step in a longer project. "It's been a long journey in a short amount of time," he said, adding that "having a competitive senior team while trying to build that pipeline simultaneously was the best path for us."

He credited support from the Bob and Rita Marley Foundation and Cedella Marley, a training camp in Florida, and Norman Powell's help in pre-qualifying. "I think the future looks pretty bright," Turner said.

Marcus McDonald, 19, played in all six games. He said the team needs to "get the youth on board" to become a top-10 team.

## What's next

Turner coaches Jamaica again at the FIBA AmeriCup 2029 pre-qualifiers in Managua, Nicaragua, against Costa Rica, Guyana and the hosts. Jamaica plays all three group games from November 27 to 29, and the top two teams advance. [See the schedule in Jamaica time](/posts/jamaica-basketball-americup-pre-qualifiers-managua).
